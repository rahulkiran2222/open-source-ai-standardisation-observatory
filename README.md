## 1. Project overview

The **Open-Source AI Standardisation Observatory (OSAISO)** is an independent research and software initiative investigating how open-source artificial intelligence projects interact with formal standardisation institutions, technical standards, knowledge networks and AI governance processes.

The project brings together ideas from:

- Innovation economics.
- Standardisation and technology policy.
- Open-source software and AI ecosystems.
- Knowledge transfer and network brokerage.
- AI governance and responsible innovation.
- Empirical research and reproducible data analysis.

Its central objective is to develop an evidence-based understanding of how technical knowledge and community-developed practices move between open-source AI ecosystems and formal standard-setting institutions.

### Central research question

> How do knowledge-network structures and documented participation in standardisation influence the diffusion and governance of open-source AI technologies?

The initial empirical investigation is deliberately narrower:

> What forms of participation by open-source AI projects in formal standardisation can be documented from public evidence, which actors connect these communities to standard-setting networks, and what knowledge-transfer pathways can be traced?

The project is being developed in stages. The current prototype demonstrates the proposed research instrument and evidence workflow. The empirical dataset, network findings and any broader assessment framework require further research and validation.

## 2. Research motivation

Open-source AI development is increasingly shaped by interactions between developers, researchers, technology companies, foundations, standards organisations and public institutions.

At the same time, technical conventions, evaluation benchmarks, model documentation practices and interoperability approaches may emerge within open-source communities before becoming formalised through standards or regulatory processes.

This raises several questions.

### 2.1 Open-source AI and formal standardisation

- Which open-source AI projects have documented participation in formal standards development?
- Do project maintainers, contributors or associated organisations participate in standard-setting activities?
- How do informal technical conventions relate to formal standards?
- What forms of participation are visible in public records?

### 2.2 Knowledge transfer and network brokerage

- Which actors connect open-source AI communities with formal standardisation institutions?
- Do intermediary actors facilitate the movement of technical knowledge into standards-development processes?
- Are there identifiable pathways between research publications, open-source technical practices, evaluation benchmarks and formal standards?
- What can network structures reveal about these relationships?

### 2.3 Innovation and diffusion

- Are documented knowledge-network relationships associated with technology diffusion or model adoption?
- Which outcomes can be measured reliably in open-source AI ecosystems?
- How do innovation pathways in open-source AI differ from those traditionally examined through firm-level patenting and standardisation research?

These are research questions, not established findings. The choice of methods and outcomes will depend on the evidence collected.

## 3. Connection to innovation economics and standardisation research

The project is motivated by research on standard-setting knowledge networks, firm innovation, open-source software and knowledge transfer.

A central theoretical interest is whether brokerage mechanisms identified in formal standard-setting networks can also be investigated in open-source AI communities.

In open-source AI, potentially relevant intermediaries include:

- Benchmark developers and evaluation communities.
- Open-source foundation representatives.
- Model and framework maintainers.
- Researchers and technical contributors.
- Standards committee participants.
- Organisations connecting research communities with formal governance processes.

The project will investigate whether these actors can be identified through documented relationships and whether their positions in the resulting networks are analytically meaningful.

The intention is not to assume that informal benchmark communities are equivalent to formal standards committees. Instead, the research will examine the similarities, differences and possible connections between them.

The Observatory is intended to support empirical research into these questions. It is not itself the theoretical contribution.

## 4. Research programme: three stages

### Stage 1 — Research instrument and evidence model

**Objective:** establish a transparent way to identify, record and classify possible relationships between open-source AI projects and formal standardisation.

Work completed in the prototype includes:

- Interactive research dashboard.
- Candidate entity and relationship model.
- SVG-based network explorer.
- Evidence register and source-linking workflow.
- Relationship-status classifications.
- Search and filtering functions.
- CSV import and export interfaces.
- Candidate keyword matching for evidence discovery.
- Research design and project briefing sections.

The current seed records are illustrative and require source-by-source audit. The prototype does not establish a representative network or verified project participation.

### Stage 2 — Empirical research design and network investigation

**Objective:** develop a reproducible empirical study of documented participation, intermediary actors and knowledge-transfer pathways.

#### 4.2.1 Evidence validation

The initial investigation will examine a bounded set of candidate relationships between open-source AI projects, technical communities, standards bodies and related institutions.

For each candidate relationship, the research will record:

- Entities and their identifiers.
- Relationship type.
- Source URL.
- Exact supporting passage, where available.
- Access date.
- Evidence category.
- Coding decision.
- Limitations and uncertainty.

Primary institutional records, committee documentation, public submissions, project governance documents and research publications will be prioritised.

The initial pilot will target approximately 5–8 candidate relationships, subject to the availability of suitable public evidence.

#### 4.2.2 Network construction

Following source validation, the research will define:

- Network boundaries.
- Units of analysis.
- Node and edge categories.
- Relevant time periods.
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Potential analytical approaches include descriptive network analysis, connected-component analysis and, where justified, brokerage measures.

Network centrality or brokerage will not be interpreted as evidence of influence without an appropriate theoretical and empirical basis.

#### 4.2.3 Knowledge-transfer analysis

The research will investigate documented pathways through which open-source AI knowledge may enter formal standardisation.

Potential evidence includes:

- Technical submissions.
- Public consultation responses.
- Working-group documentation.
- Standards-related publications.
- Research outputs cited in standards.
- Documented liaison and institutional relationships.

The research will distinguish direct evidence of knowledge transfer from conceptual relevance or temporal association.

#### 4.2.4 Innovation and diffusion outcomes

Where reliable longitudinal data are available, the research may investigate associations between documented network relationships and selected innovation or diffusion outcomes.

Potential outcomes require further evaluation and could include:

- Documented model adoption.
- Technology integration.
- Interoperability developments.
- Reuse and diffusion across projects.
- Other observable innovation indicators.

Repository popularity, downloads, stars and activity will not be treated as direct measures of innovation or adoption without a defensible operational definition.

Patent measures will be considered only if they are theoretically appropriate to the specific research question.

#### 4.2.5 Reproducible analytical pipeline

Potential technical developments include:

- Formal data schema.
- Data validation and duplicate detection.
- Entity resolution.
- Source provenance records.
- Network construction from validated evidence.
- Reproducible Python analysis.
- Sensitivity analysis.
- Versioned CSV and JSON datasets.
- Documented analytical notebooks.

These capabilities are planned extensions, not claims about the current prototype.

### Stage 3 — European Observatory and wider research infrastructure

**Long-term research ambition:** develop the Observatory into a sustained research infrastructure for understanding open-source AI, standardisation, innovation and governance.

Possible directions include:

1. Expand the evidence base across European and international open-source AI projects.
2. Map relationships between open-source communities, standards organisations, research institutions and public authorities.
3. Investigate differences in participation across regions, institutional settings and technology domains.
4. Develop a reproducible longitudinal dataset.
5. Publish comparative research and policy analysis.
6. Establish research collaborations with standards bodies, open-source foundations, universities and public institutions.

The European scope would provide an initial institutional and policy setting. International coverage could be developed through comparable data collection and collaboration.

The long-term aim is to make the Observatory useful beyond a single research project or doctoral thesis.

## 5. Long-term possibility: an evidence-based open-source AI assessment framework

A possible future extension is to investigate whether open-source AI projects can be compared through a transparent, multidimensional assessment framework.

The proposed framework would not simply rank projects by popularity or produce a single opaque AI safety score.

Potential assessment dimensions include:

| Dimension | Research interest |
|---|---|
| Openness and reproducibility | Code, model artefacts, documentation, reproducibility and licensing |
| Security and maintenance | Vulnerability disclosure, maintenance practices and remediation records |
| Evaluation and limitations | Benchmark documentation, independent evaluation and disclosed limitations |
| Standards engagement | Documented participation in standardisation and technical governance |
| Project governance | Decision-making, contribution processes and organisational accountability |
| Privacy and data stewardship | Data provenance, privacy documentation and relevant safeguards |

Each dimension would require:

- Explicit operational definitions.
- Traceable evidence.
- Consistent coding rules.
- Missing-data and uncertainty treatment.
- Independent validation.
- Periodic reassessment.

A lack of public evidence should not automatically be interpreted as evidence of poor safety or non-compliance.

The framework would not replace independent security audits, legal assessments, AI conformity assessments or regulatory supervision.

### Potential contribution

If empirically validated, this approach could support:

- Comparative research on open-source AI project practices.
- Evidence-based policy analysis.
- Better visibility into project governance and documentation.
- Research on the relationship between standardisation and innovation.
- More transparent evaluation of open-source AI ecosystems.

This is a proposed future research direction. No validated index or project ranking currently exists within the Observatory.

## 6. Methodological principles

The project follows the following principles.

**Source traceability:** Every substantive relationship should be linked to evidence that can be inspected.

**Separation of observation and interpretation:** A source-backed fact should be distinguishable from a researcher's interpretation.

**Explicit uncertainty:** Missing or incomplete public evidence should be recorded rather than converted into unsupported conclusions.

**Reproducible coding:** Inclusion rules and relationship categories should be documented.

**Independent validation:** Where feasible, a second reviewer should assess a subset of coded records.

**Temporal awareness:** Relationships and project governance arrangements can change. Records should include dates and version histories.

**Responsible assessment:** Scores and indicators should communicate their scope and limitations and should not be presented as universal guarantees of safety or compliance.

## 7. Current prototype

The live prototype provides five main views:

1. **Overview:** research motivation and prototype indicators.
2. **Network explorer:** candidate entities and provisional relationships.
3. **Evidence register:** source-linked records, filters and CSV workflows.
4. **Research design:** scope, coding and methodological workflow.
5. **Project briefing:** research explanation and methodological limitations.

The application currently uses HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript, SVG and CSV-based data workflows, with static hosting through GitHub Pages.

It is designed as a lightweight proof of concept. A persistent database, automated collection system and analytical backend may be introduced when justified by the research requirements.

## 11. Data quality, copyright and research ethics

The Observatory will link to lawful source locations and store appropriate metadata and supporting excerpts where permitted.

It will not republish copyrighted standards documents or restricted material without permission.

The project is not:

- An official standards certification service.
- A legal compliance checker.
- A substitute for AI safety evaluation.
- An automated determination of GDPR or AI Act compliance.
- An endorsement or approval system for AI projects.

Any future public assessment framework will require clear methodology, transparent uncertainty reporting, correction procedures and independent review.

## 14. Project status and acknowledgement

The Open-Source AI Standardisation Observatory is an independent research and software initiative.

It is not an official TU Berlin, Fraunhofer ISI, DIN, DKE or standards-development organisation project. References to researchers and institutions identify research context and do not imply endorsement, collaboration or supervision.

The project is intended to evolve through academic feedback, systematic evidence collection, methodological validation and reproducible research.

**Current status: Stage 2 research design and prototype development. Empirical verification and substantive network analysis remain outstanding.**
